## 2.2. Effects on Health, Environment and the Problems of the Age Reflected in the Field of Engineering

Online toxicity represents one of the defining sociotechnical problems of the current era. Sustained exposure to cyberbullying, identity-based harassment, and threatening messages is clinically linked to anxiety disorders, depression, and in severe cases, suicidal ideation — particularly among adolescent users. The engineering motivation behind ToxicGuard is to serve as a technical intervention in this documented public health problem, detecting harmful content before it reaches its targets and reducing the psychological harm load on vulnerable digital citizens.

Beyond individual health, rampant online toxicity degrades the broader informational environment — the shared digital space in which public discourse takes place. Academic research has also established a link between unchecked online hate speech and real-world radicalization, making moderation tools a matter not just of platform health but of broader social safety. From an engineering perspective, developing AI systems that are simultaneously accurate, fair, and transparent in this domain is one of the defining technical challenges of this generation, requiring professionals to engage directly with algorithmic bias, fairness metrics, and the societal consequences of automated decision-making.
