# 3. REALISTIC CONSTRAINTS AND CONDITIONS

## 3.1. Sustainable Development Goal

The development of the ToxicGuard automated toxicity detection system intrinsically aligns with several critical objectives outlined by the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), most notably Goal 16: Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions. A fundamental target within Goal 16 emphasizes the urgent need to significantly reduce all forms of violence and related death rates everywhere. In the modern era, the digital public square represents a massive extension of our societal infrastructure, and unfortunately, it has become a prevalent vector for psychological violence, targeted harassment, and the incubation of extremist ideologies. By engineering robust computational tools capable of rapidly identifying and mitigating severe cyberbullying, hate speech, and explicit threats of physical harm, this project actively contributes to creating safer, more peaceful digital environments. 

Furthermore, the project directly supports Goal 3: Good Health and Well-being. Peer-reviewed psychological research consistently demonstrates a strong correlation between sustained exposure to online toxicity and significantly elevated rates of anxiety, clinical depression, and diminished mental health among internet users, particularly among vulnerable adolescent populations. By successfully filtering malicious vitriol before it reaches targeted individuals, automated moderation acts as a preventative public health mechanism, directly protecting the psychological resilience and overall well-being of digital citizens. Finally, the system indirectly bolsters Goal 5: Gender Equality. Online harassment disproportionately targets women and minorities, often utilizing identity-based hate to intimidate and silence them in public forums. By explicitly training the machine learning models to detect and accurately flag "identity hate" modifiers and misogynistic obscenities, the project promotes a more equitable, inclusive digital space where historically marginalized groups can participate fully in civic discourse without the paralyzing fear of gender-based electronic violence.
